ESP32-C6 & 802.15.4 a hands-on tutorial in ten chapters

Listening to 802.15.4 with an ESP32-C6

The ESP32-C6 has a radio most people never switch on. Underneath Zigbee, Thread and Matter sits plain IEEE 802.15.4, and the chip will let you talk to it directly — which means you can build a packet sniffer, work out what the devices around you are saying, and then say it back to them. This tutorial does all three, one flashable sketch at a time.

It is built from a real project: six laser-safety monitors in a building, wired to Digi XBee radios, beaconing status to an indicator box in a format nobody had written down. Chapters 1 through 9 are the techniques. Chapter 10 is how that protocol actually fell, including the two places the obvious reading of the spec turned out to be wrong.

Who this is for

You have used an Arduino before and can read C. No RF background is assumed — the radio concepts are introduced where they are needed. No Zigbee or Thread stack is involved anywhere; this is the raw MAC layer.

What you will have built

By chapter 6 you have a scanner that sweeps all sixteen channels and reports where the traffic is:

[ch 21 / 0x15] listening...
  ch 21  len 14  rssi  -63 dBm  lqi  11  | 61 88 46 01 0B 00 00 01 00 83 00 0B C1 0B
  ch 21  len  5  rssi  -62 dBm  lqi   9  | 02 00 46 C1 09

---- frames per channel so far ----
  ch 14 (0x0E, 2420 MHz): 56
  ch 21 (0x15, 2455 MHz): 119
  ch 25 (0x19, 2475 MHz): 66

By chapter 7 the sketch reads those bytes back to you as fields, and by chapter 10 it reads them as English:

monitor 1: laser ON    (pan 0x0B01 src 0x0001 seq 0x46 rssi -63 dBm)
monitor 2: laser off   (pan 0x0B01 src 0x0002 seq 0x88 rssi -62 dBm)

Hardware

ItemNotes
Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6 What this tutorial was written on. Any ESP32-C6 board works; adjust the LED pin in chapter 1.
USB-C cable A data cable. Charge-only cables are a classic hour lost.
A second C6 (optional) Only chapter 9 wants one, so you can watch your own transmissions from outside.
Something transmitting (optional) Any Zigbee, Thread or XBee 802.15.4 device nearby. Without one the receive chapters run but stay quiet — chapter 9 can supply your own traffic.
Chip support

802.15.4 hardware exists on the ESP32-C6, C5 and H2. It does not exist on the original ESP32, the S2, S3, C2 or C3 — on those boards esp_ieee802154.h will not even be found. No amount of library hunting fixes that; the radio is not on the die.

Software setup

  1. Install the Arduino IDE 2.x.
  2. File → Preferences → Additional board manager URLs, add: 
    https://espressif.github.io/arduino-esp32/package_esp32_index.json
  3. Tools → Board → Boards Manager, search esp32, install version 3.0.0 or later. Core 2.x does not support the C6 and does not have the buffer-release function chapter 4 depends on.
  4. Tools → Board → ESP32 Arduino → XIAO_ESP32C6.
  5. Tools → USB CDC On Boot: Enabled. Without this the Serial Monitor stays empty and everything looks broken. Chapter 2 is about this.

Getting the sketches

Each chapter has one sketch, in its own folder so the Arduino IDE will open it. Clone the repository and open them from docs/sketches/:

git clone https://github.com/JenuC/esp32c6-tests.git
cd esp32c6-tests/docs/sketches
# then File > Open... > ch01_blink/ch01_blink.ino

Or download any single sketch from the link at the top of its chapter. The sketches are heavily commented and stand on their own — the chapter pages are the narrative around them, not a transcript of them.

The chapters

  1. 01Blink Prove the toolchain works before blaming the radio for anything.
  2. 02Serial you can trust USB CDC, the while (!Serial) trap, and why a heartbeat line saves hours.
  3. 03First frames out of the air Enable the radio, go promiscuous, print raw packets. Contains a deliberate bug.
  4. 04The twenty frame wall Why chapter 3 dies after exactly 20 frames, and the one line that fixes it.
  5. 05Getting out of the callback The callback is an ISR. Hand frames to loop() through a lock-free ring buffer.
  6. 06Scanning the band Sweep channels 11–26, find the traffic, and understand what a scanner cannot see.
  7. 07Reading the header Parse 802.15.4 properly: the FCF, addressing modes, and PAN ID compression.
  8. 08The two bytes that are not a CRC The frame ends with RSSI and LQI, not the FCS. Proved on-device from live traffic.
  9. 09Transmitting, and getting acknowledged Build a frame by hand. An ACK is the only honest proof you got the header right.
  10. 10Decoding an unknown payload The method, and the real case study: three mystery bytes, 12,800 captured frames.

The frame, for reference

This is the shape everything in the tutorial is working towards. One real frame from the captures, split into its parts:

61 88FCF
46seq
01 0Bdst PAN
00 00dst addr
01 00src addr
83 00 0Bpayload
C1 0Brssi / lqi

Chapters 3–7 get you the header. Chapter 8 explains why those last two bytes are not the checksum the spec says they are. Chapter 10 cracks the three in the middle.
